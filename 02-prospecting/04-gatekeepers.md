# Gatekeepers

A gatekeeper is anyone who answers the phone but is not the decision maker: an office manager, a receptionist, a spouse who does the books, a crew member who happened to pick up. In small home-service businesses, the "gatekeeper" is often the owner's most trusted person. Treat them accordingly.

## The right mindset

The gatekeeper is not your enemy. Their job is literally to protect the owner's time from time-wasters, and most of the calls they screen deserve to be screened. Your job is to not sound like those calls.

Two things get you through more than any trick:

1. **Be pleasant.** Warm, unhurried, human. Use their name once you have it. Gatekeepers get talked past and talked down to all day; the caller who treats them like a person stands out immediately.
2. **Be direct.** Say who you are and what you want in plain words. Hedging, fake familiarity, and vagueness are exactly what screening filters catch. Confidence and clarity read as "this call is legitimate."

Also: a gatekeeper is a source, not just an obstacle. They can tell you the owner's name, when he is reachable, and whether the business even buys what you sell. A friendly 60-second chat with the gatekeeper is a win even if you never reach the owner that day.

## Example lines

Adjust the wording to sound like you. The structure is what matters: pleasant, direct, specific.

1. **The straightforward ask:**
"Hi, this is [YOUR NAME] from [YOUR COMPANY]. Is Mike around? It's about the company's website."

Short, calm, uses the owner's name if you have it. Do not over-explain before being asked.

2. **When they ask "what's this regarding?":**
"Sure, fair question. I noticed the website doesn't work well on phones, and since most of your customers search on a phone, I wanted to ask Mike if that's something he's aware of. Is he in?"

Answer honestly and specifically, then re-ask. A real reason, stated plainly, passes most screens.

3. **When you do not have the owner's name:**
"Hi, I'll be honest, this is a cold call, but a quick and relevant one. Who's the right person to talk to about how you get customers online, and are they in today?"

Admitting it is a cold call is disarming precisely because nobody does it. It trades a small amount of leverage for a lot of trust.

4. **When the owner is out:**
"No problem. When's he usually easiest to catch, mornings or late afternoon? And can I ask your name so I can tell him we spoke?"

You leave with the two things you came for: a time and a name. Log both on your call sheet.

5. **Recruiting the gatekeeper:**
"Dana, you probably know better than anyone: does the company get most of its work from word of mouth, or does the website actually bring in calls?"

Asking their opinion turns a screener into a source, and sometimes into an advocate who walks the phone back to the owner.

6. **The callback close:**
"Totally understand he's slammed. If I call back Thursday around 8, before the crews head out, is that usually a good time? Great, I'll ask for you when I do."

You are setting your own callback and making an ally in one move.

## What never to do

Do not lie or trick. Not because it never works short-term, but because it always costs you long-term, and you are building a local reputation in a small market.

- **Never pretend to know the owner.** No "just tell him it's Jack, he'll know what it's about." The moment the owner picks up confused, you are done, and the gatekeeper will remember you.
- **Never claim it is a personal or urgent matter** when it is a sales call.
- **Never pretend to be returning their call** or following up on a conversation that did not happen.
- **Never be rude, dismissive, or try to steamroll** the gatekeeper. They talk to the owner every day; you are hoping to talk to him once. Guess whose account of the call sticks.
- **Never bad-mouth the business to the gatekeeper.** "Your website is embarrassing" said to the person who may have built it does not go well. Frame gaps as opportunities.

One honest line beats every trick: "It's a sales call, but a relevant one, and it'll take him two minutes to know if he cares." Owners of trade businesses are salespeople themselves. Most respect straight shooters and have zero patience for games.

## Log it

Every gatekeeper contact goes on your call sheet: status **gatekeeper**, plus their name and the best time to reach the owner in the notes. Next call, you open with "Hi Dana, it's Jack again," and you are no longer a stranger.
