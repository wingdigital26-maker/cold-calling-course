# Building a List

You do not need to buy leads. For local home-service businesses (roofers, HVAC, plumbers, landscapers), everything you need is public and free. What you need is a repeatable process for finding them, capturing the right fields, and keeping the list clean.

## Free sources

### 1. Google Maps
Your main source. Search "[trade] [city]", for example "roofer Fort Worth". Every result gives you the business name, phone number, website (or lack of one), review count, and rating. Work the map in a grid: search the same trade in each suburb, not just the big city, because smaller towns have less-contacted businesses.

### 2. Business directories
Yelp, Angi, Thumbtack, HomeAdvisor, and the Better Business Bureau all list local trades. These are useful for cross-checking phone numbers and for finding businesses that are paying for leads, which tells you they spend money on marketing. BBB profiles sometimes list the owner's name, which Google Maps almost never does.

### 3. Chamber of Commerce lists
Most local Chambers publish a public member directory on their website. Members are established businesses that pay dues, and the listings often include the owner or a contact person by name. Search "[city] chamber of commerce member directory".

### 4. LinkedIn
Search the company name to find who owns or runs it. You often will not find small trade businesses here, but when you do, you get the owner's name and sometimes how long they have been in business. Use it to identify the decision maker, not to build the list itself.

### 5. OpenStreetMap
A free, open map database. Businesses tagged in OSM can be exported in bulk (look up the Overpass Turbo tool if you want to get technical). Useful for finding businesses that do not show up prominently on Google, which often means they have weak online presence, which is exactly who you may want to call.

## What fields to capture

Keep it simple. One row per business:

- **Business name**
- **Owner name** (if you can find it; blank is fine, you will fill it in later)
- **Phone number**
- **Website URL** (or "none")
- **Review count** (Google)
- **Rating** (Google)
- **City**
- **Source** (where you found them)
- **Notes** (anything odd you spotted)

A spreadsheet is fine. Do not overbuild this.

## Why review count and website quality matter

These two fields are your targeting signals. They tell you, before you ever dial, what problem the business likely has and whether they can afford you.

**Review count** is a rough proxy for how established and busy a business is:

- **0 to 15 reviews**: newer or invisible online. They likely need marketing help the most, but some are one-person operations with no budget. Easier to reach the owner directly.
- **Roughly 15 to 100 reviews**: established, doing real revenue, but usually still with obvious gaps online. Often the sweet spot.
- **Hundreds of reviews**: doing well. Harder to sell "you need more visibility" to, but they may buy other services.

These bands are rough guidance from field experience, not statistics. Adjust them for your market.

**Website quality** tells you what to pitch. No website at all, a Facebook page instead of a site, a site that is broken on mobile, or a site last touched years ago: each one is a concrete, checkable problem you can name on the call. "I looked at your website" lands very differently when you can say exactly what is wrong with it.

A business with 40 reviews and a terrible website is a better prospect than a business with 400 reviews and a polished one. Sort your list accordingly.

## List hygiene

A dirty list wastes call time and makes you sound sloppy.

**Dedupe.** The same business shows up under slightly different names across sources ("Smith Roofing" vs "Smith Roofing LLC"). Before calling, sort by phone number and remove duplicates. Phone number is the best dedupe key because names vary and numbers rarely do.

**Verify numbers.** Google Maps numbers are usually current, but directories go stale. If a number looks off, check it against the business's own website. Disconnected numbers and wrong numbers are normal; mark them dead in your sheet and move on rather than deleting the row, so you do not re-add them next month.

**Check they are still in business.** Recent reviews (within the last few months) are a good sign of life. A business with no reviews in two years and a dead website may be gone.

**Refresh, do not hoard.** A list decays. Build in batches of 50 to 100, call through them, then build the next batch. Do not spend a week building a 1,000-row list before making your first call.
