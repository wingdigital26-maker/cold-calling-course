# Pre-Call Research

Research is what separates a cold call from a spam call. But it has to be fast. If you spend 20 minutes researching every prospect, you will make 10 calls a day and burn out. The target is 2 minutes per prospect, done in a batch before your call block, not between dials.

## The 2-minute routine

Set a timer if you have to. Three stops, then you are done.

### 1. Website (about 45 seconds)
Open their site if they have one. You are looking for:

- Does it exist? Is it a real site or just a Facebook page?
- Does it look current or abandoned?
- Does it work on mobile? (Open it on your phone or shrink the browser.)
- Is there a clear way to contact them or request a quote?
- Any names? "About us" pages and footers sometimes name the owner.

No website is itself a finding. Write "no site" and move on.

### 2. Google reviews (about 45 seconds)
Open their Google Business profile.

- Review count and rating (you may already have this from list-building).
- Read the two or three most recent reviews. What do customers praise? Speed, cleanup, honesty?
- Any recent negative review the owner replied to? How they reply tells you about them.
- When was the last review? Months of silence can mean slow season or slow business.
- Does the owner's name appear in reviews or replies? Customers often say "Mike and his crew were great." Now you know it is Mike.

### 3. Socials (about 30 seconds)
Quick look at Facebook and Instagram if they have them.

- Are they posting? Job photos, before-and-afters?
- Last post date. An account dead for a year is a talking point.
- Owner's name often appears here when it appears nowhere else.

## What to note

You are not writing an essay. Three or four short lines per prospect:

- Owner name (or "unknown")
- One specific, true observation (the hook)
- The gap you would fix (what you would pitch)
- Anything to avoid (recent bad press, they already have an agency, etc.)

## How research turns into an opener

The point of all this is one sentence at the top of your call. A generic opener sounds like every other sales call the owner got this week. A researched opener proves you actually looked.

Generic: "Hi, I help roofing companies get more leads online."

Researched: "Hi, is this Mike? I was looking at Example Roofing's reviews, you have a 4.9 with people raving about your cleanup crew, but when I searched 'roofer in Springfield' you were nowhere on the first page. That gap is the reason I'm calling."

The formula: **one true specific compliment or observation + one specific gap + that is why I am calling.** The observation earns you the next 20 seconds. The gap gives the call a reason to exist.

## Example prospect card

Here is a filled-in card for an obviously fictional business:

---

**Business:** Example Roofing Co
**City:** Springfield
**Phone:** 555-0142
**Owner:** Mike Exampleton (named in 3 review replies, signs off Facebook posts as "Mike")
**Website:** examples-roofing-fake.example - exists but not mobile friendly, no quote form, copyright says 2019
**Reviews:** 47 on Google, 4.9 rating, last review 3 weeks ago. Customers repeatedly praise fast tarping after storms and cleanup.
**Socials:** Facebook active until 14 months ago, last post is a crew photo. No Instagram.
**Hook:** 4.9 rating with 47 reviews but invisible in search for "roofer Springfield" and site is unusable on a phone.
**Pitch angle:** Their reputation is already great; the online presence just does not show it. Fix the site, get them ranking, turn the reviews into leads.
**Avoid:** One recent 1-star review about scheduling; do not bring it up, but do not be surprised if he is touchy about it.
**Opener draft:** "Hi, is this Mike? Quick one: I was reading Example Roofing's reviews, people love how fast you tarp after storms. But your site basically does not work on a phone, and most of your customers are searching on one. That is why I'm calling."

---

That card took 2 minutes to build and gives you everything you need: the right name, a real compliment, a real gap, and a first sentence that no other caller that week will have.

## Batch it

Do research for your whole call block before you start dialing: 20 to 30 cards in an hour, then dial straight through. Switching between research mode and calling mode on every dial kills momentum.
