# The 10 Most Common Objections

Every response below follows the framework from file 01: acknowledge, clarify, reframe, ask. Say them out loud until they sound like you, not like a script. Adjust the words to your own voice. Never say something you can't back up.

Context for the examples: you run an agency calling local home-service businesses (roofers, plumbers, HVAC, junk removal, etc.) about their online presence and lead flow.

---

## 1. "Not interested"

**What it really means:** Usually nothing. It's the reflex. They heard "sales call" and swatted. Nine times out of ten they don't yet know what they're not interested in.

**Response A:**
"Totally fair, you didn't know I was calling. Before I let you go, can I ask one thing? When someone in [city] searches for a roofer right now, do you know where you show up? That's all this is about. Worth 15 minutes to see it?"

**Response B:**
"No problem. Quick question so I don't bug you again: is it that you've got more work than you can handle, or just not a good moment? ... Got it. If I could show you the two or three jobs a month you're probably losing to [competitor], would that be worth a short look?"

**What NOT to say:** "Why not?" (sounds defensive and puts them on trial), or launching back into your pitch louder and faster. They said not interested to a pitch. More pitch is more of the thing they rejected.

---

## 2. "I'm too busy" / "This is a bad time"

**What it really means:** Sometimes literally true. They're on a roof, in a crawlspace, or with a customer. Sometimes it's a soft brush-off. Either way, respect the time and win the next call.

**Response A:**
"I hear you, and I'll be quick. I work with [trade] companies on the leads side, and busy is actually the best time to fix this, because slow season is coming either way. Can I grab 15 minutes Thursday instead of doing this now?"

**Response B:**
"No worries at all, sounds like I caught you mid-job. When's usually calmer for you, mornings or end of day? ... Great, I'll call you tomorrow at 8, and if it's still crazy just tell me and I'm gone."

**What NOT to say:** "It'll only take a minute" and then talking for five. That lie costs you the callback. Also don't say "I'll be brief" and keep pitching. If they're busy, your only goal is a scheduled next touch.

---

## 3. "Send me an email"

**What it really means:** The polite hang-up. Cold emails to someone who asked for one out of politeness get deleted. Occasionally it's genuine, so don't treat it as an insult. Treat it as a fork: qualify it.

**Response A:**
"Happy to. So I send you something actually useful instead of a brochure, what should it cover? Is it the leads side or the website side that would matter more? ... Perfect. And once you've had a look, does Tuesday or Wednesday work for a quick call about it?"

**Response B:**
"Sure, I can do that. Honest question though: is email genuinely how you'd look at this, or is that your polite way of saying no? Either answer's fine, I'd just rather know than clutter your inbox."

**What NOT to say:** "Emails just get ignored, so let's talk now." You're calling their bluff in an aggressive way and telling them their suggestion is dumb. Also don't just say "sure!" and hang up. An email with no agreed next step is a dead lead.

---

## 4. "We already have someone" / "We already work with an agency"

**What it really means:** Could be true and they're happy. Could be true and they're lukewarm. Could be their brother-in-law who made the site in 2016. This objection is an invitation to ask exactly one good question.

**Response A:**
"That's good to hear, honestly, most guys I call have nobody. Out of curiosity, how's it going? Are they getting you actual booked jobs, or is it more the website and posts kind of thing?"

**Response B:**
"Makes sense, and I'm not calling to trash whoever you're with. A lot of owners I talk to keep their current setup and just use a second opinion once a year to keep everyone honest. Would a free look at where you rank be useful, even if nothing changes?"

**What NOT to say:** Anything negative about their current provider. They chose that provider, so insulting them insults the owner's judgment. And never claim you can definitely beat results you haven't seen.

---

## 5. "How much is it?"

**What it really means:** Interest, actually. Nobody asks the price of things they don't want. But quoting a number cold turns you into a commodity to be compared, and without knowing their situation any number you give is a guess.

**Response A:**
"Fair question. It depends on what you actually need, and I'd be guessing right now. That's exactly what the 15 minutes is for: I look at what you've got, tell you what I'd do and what it costs, and you decide. If the number doesn't make sense, you tell me no and we're done. Does Tuesday work?"

**Response B:**
"Depends on scope, but I'll shoot straight: most of the [trade] companies I work with are in the [honest range] a month range. Whether that makes sense for you depends on what a booked job is worth to you. Want to run those numbers together for 15 minutes?"

**What NOT to say:** "Don't worry about the price." They're the one paying, of course they'll worry about it. Dodging entirely reads as hiding something. Either bridge to the meeting or give an honest range, but never lowball a fake number to get the meeting.

---

## 6. "Call me later" / "Call me next month"

**What it really means:** Sometimes real (mid-season, mid-crisis). Often a soft no they hope you'll forget. The difference between the two is whether they'll commit to a specific time.

**Response A:**
"Sure thing. So I'm not just cold-calling you again, let's put a real time on it. How's Tuesday the 9th, morning or afternoon? ... Morning, great. I'll text you a reminder the day before."

**Response B:**
"Happy to. Quick gut check first so I'm not wasting your time next month: is this a 'genuinely bad month' thing, or a 'probably never' thing? If it's never, just tell me, no hard feelings."

**What NOT to say:** "You said that last time." Even if true, it's a guilt trip and guilt trips end relationships. And don't accept a vague "yeah sometime later" as a win. Vague later means never. Pin a date or get an honest no.

---

## 7. "Who is this?" / "How did you get my number?"

**What it really means:** Suspicion, and fair enough. Robocalls have ruined the phone. They want to know you're a real person before anything else. Answer plainly and fast, because dodging here kills all trust.

**Response A:**
"Good question, sorry, should've led with that. I'm [name] with [company], we're local here in [city]. Your number's on your Google listing, which is actually part of why I called. Got 30 seconds for the reason?"

**Response B:**
"I'm [name], I run a small agency in [city] that works with [trade] companies. I found you on Google while looking at who shows up for [trade] around here. You came up on page two, and that's costing you calls. That's the whole reason I rang."

**What NOT to say:** Anything evasive, like "that's not important" or a fake claim that someone referred you. One lie detected and the call is over, plus your name travels in a small trade community. Answer the question directly, then earn the next 30 seconds.

---

## 8. "We're slow right now" / "Money's tight"

**What it really means:** A real constraint, and also the exact problem you solve. Handle this one gently. Slow is stressful, and a pushy pitch to a stressed owner feels gross to both of you.

**Response A:**
"I appreciate you being straight with me. Slow is honestly the reason I call at all, because slow usually isn't a work-quality problem, it's a being-found problem. I'm not going to push you to spend money in a tight month. Can I at least show you, free, where the calls are going instead of to you? No cost to look."

**Response B:**
"That's fair, and I won't pretend a marketing bill is fun when things are slow. Let me ask it this way: what's one booked job worth to you, roughly? ... Okay, so if this pays for itself at one or two jobs a month, it might be worth 15 minutes to check the math. If it doesn't add up, don't buy it."

**What NOT to say:** "You can't afford NOT to." That line is the sound of every huckster they've ever hung up on. Never use fear or shame about their slow season, and never promise leads you can't guarantee.

---

## 9. "I need to think about it"

**What it really means:** At the cold-call stage this usually means there's a question or doubt they haven't said out loud, or the ask felt bigger than it is. Your job is to find the real hesitation, not to pressure the thinking away.

**Response A:**
"Of course, take your time. Can I ask, what's the main thing you'd be weighing? Sometimes I can answer it in a minute and save you the thinking. ... And just to be clear, this is 15 minutes of looking at your listing together, not signing anything."

**Response B:**
"Totally reasonable. How about this: I'll email you the three things we'd cover so you can think about something concrete, and I'll pencil in Thursday. If you look at it and it's a no, reply 'no thanks' and I'll leave you alone. Deal?"

**What NOT to say:** "What's there to think about?" (dismissive), or the old-school "if I could show you X, would you sign today?" pressure closes. You're booking a 15-minute chat, not selling a car. Make the ask feel as small as it actually is.

---

## 10. "I'm not the decision maker" / "You'd have to talk to my husband / the owner"

**What it really means:** Take it at face value. You're probably talking to office staff, a crew member, or a co-owner. This person is not an obstacle. They are the door, and how you treat them decides whether the door opens.

**Response A:**
"Ah, got it, thanks for telling me. Who would be the right person, and what's the best way to reach them? ... And can I mention you were kind enough to point me the right way?"

**Response B:**
"No problem. Can I ask you one thing anyway, since you're there every day: is getting enough calls something you all ever talk about? ... Interesting. When I catch [owner], I'll keep it to 15 minutes. Is morning or afternoon better for reaching him?"

**What NOT to say:** "Well, can you just put him on?" (bulldozing the gatekeeper), or hanging up abruptly the second you realize they can't buy. They talk to the owner every day. Be the pleasant call they mention, not the rude one.

---

## The thread through all ten

Notice what every good response has in common: it stays calm, it asks a question instead of arguing, it tells the truth, and it ends with a small specific ask. Notice what every "what not to say" has in common: pressure, guilt, dodging, or a lie. That's the whole game.
