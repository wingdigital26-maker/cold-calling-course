# The Objection Framework

Every objection you will ever hear can be handled with the same four-step pattern. Learn this once and you never have to memorize scripts in a panic again.

## The pattern

1. **Acknowledge**
2. **Clarify or isolate**
3. **Reframe**
4. **Ask again**

That's it. Let's walk through each step.

### 1. Acknowledge

Agree with their right to feel that way before you do anything else. Not with the objection itself, but with the person.

- "Totally fair."
- "Yeah, I get that. You didn't know I was calling."
- "That makes sense."

This does two things. It signals you are not going to fight them, which lowers their guard. And it buys you two seconds to think.

What acknowledgment is NOT: "I understand, BUT..." The word "but" erases everything before it. Use "and" or just pause instead.

### 2. Clarify or isolate

Most objections are vague on purpose. "Not interested" could mean "I'm cooking dinner," "I got burned by the last agency," or "I have no idea what you just said." You cannot answer a vague objection, so make it specific.

- "Just so I don't waste your time, is it the timing, or is this just not something you'd ever look at?"
- "Fair enough. Can I ask, is it that you're happy with how things are, or you've just never thought about it?"

Isolating means checking if this is the only thing in the way: "If the timing worked, would this be worth 15 minutes?" If they say no, there's another objection hiding behind the first one. Better to find it now.

### 3. Reframe

Now, and only now, offer a different way to look at it. Short. One or two sentences. You are not delivering a rebuttal, you are opening a door.

- Objection: "We're too busy." Reframe: "That's actually why I called. Busy usually means the phone's ringing, and this is about keeping it that way when the season slows down."

### 4. Ask again

An objection handled without a new ask is just a pleasant conversation. Always come back to a small, specific request.

- "Would Tuesday morning work for a quick 15 minutes?"

Ask once more, maybe twice across the whole call. If you're on your third loop, you're pushing, and pushing loses. See file 04.

## Why arguing loses

The moment you argue, the call stops being about their business and starts being about who wins. Nobody has ever been debated into a meeting. Even if you "win" the point, they now feel cornered, and cornered people say no just to get their power back.

Your job is not to prove them wrong. Your job is to be the one calm, reasonable person who called them today.

## The first objection is a reflex, not an answer

Business owners get hammered with sales calls. "Not interested" and "we're all set" are the verbal equivalent of swatting a fly. They said it before their brain processed who you are or what you do. It's a reflex against being sold to, not a decision about your offer.

That's why the framework works. Acknowledge and clarify get you past the reflex to the real person. Once you're talking to the real person, you find out whether there's an actual objection underneath. Often there isn't.

One rule to carry through everything: you get to gently work past the reflex once, maybe twice. A real, considered no is a no. Respect it, and read file 04 on how to leave well.
