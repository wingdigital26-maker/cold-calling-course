# Booking the Meeting

The whole call exists for this moment. Not to sell your service, not to explain everything you do. Just to trade a phone conversation for a scheduled 15 minutes. Here's how to do it cleanly and how to make sure the meeting actually happens.

## The assumptive time offer

Don't ask IF they want to meet. Ask WHEN. Weak closers ask permission twice: once to meet, once to pick a time. That's two chances to say no.

**Weak:** "Would you maybe be open to setting up a call sometime?"

**Strong:** "Let's grab 15 minutes. Does Tuesday morning work, or is Thursday afternoon better?"

Offering two specific options does the picking for them. Either answer books the meeting. This is not a trick, and it isn't pushy as long as the conversation earned it. If they've shown zero interest, an assumptive close is just annoying. If they've engaged at all, it's simply confident.

If neither time works, hand them the wheel: "No problem, what does your week look like?" The goal is a specific date and time before you hang up. "Sometime next week" is not a booking. It's a polite goodbye.

## Keep the ask small

You are asking for 15 minutes. Say the number out loud, and mean it.

- "This is 15 minutes, and I watch the clock."
- "If we hit 15 and you want to keep going, that's your call, not mine."

Why 15? Because it's small enough that saying yes costs them almost nothing, and it forces you to be sharp. A vague "quick call" sounds like an hour of being sold to. A named, small number sounds safe.

Also keep the CONTENT of the ask small. You're not asking them to buy, switch providers, or make a decision. Say so: "You're not deciding anything on this call. I'll show you what I found, you tell me if it's interesting." Small ask, honest frame, easy yes.

## Confirm it on the calendar, immediately

A meeting that only exists in your head does not exist.

While still on the phone:

1. **Repeat it back.** "So that's Tuesday the 9th at 9:30, I'll call you at this number."
2. **Get their email.** "What's the best email? I'll send a calendar invite so it's on your phone." Getting the email also quietly confirms they're serious.
3. **Send the invite before you dial the next number.** Not at end of day. Now. Title it something they'll recognize: "Jack / Wing Digital, 15 min re: [Company] leads."

If they hesitate on giving an email, don't fight it. Confirm by text instead: "I'll text you the details right now so it's in your phone."

## Reducing no-shows

Cold-booked meetings have brutal no-show rates. By meeting day, you're a stranger from a phone call they barely remember. Fight that with three touches:

1. **The invite** (immediately after the call, as above). An accepted invite is your strongest signal. If it sits unaccepted, that's a flag.
2. **A short human text or email the day before or morning of.** Same-day confirmation is the single biggest no-show killer in my experience. Keep it human, not corporate:
   - "Hey Mike, Jack here. Still good for 3:30 today? Just need you and 15 minutes."
   - If they reply "yes," they'll almost always show. If they reply "actually, today's bad," great, you just saved a slot. Rebook on the spot: "No sweat. Tomorrow same time?"
3. **A reason to show up.** People show up when there's something waiting for them. "I'll have your Google rankings pulled up and two things I'd fix first" beats "looking forward to chatting."

If they no-show anyway: don't sulk, don't guilt. Call once about 10 minutes in: "Hey, it's Jack, we had 3:30 on the books. Did I catch you at a bad time?" Then one rebook attempt. Two no-shows with no reply means treat it as a no and move on gracefully (see file 04).

## What to do when they book

The seconds after "yeah, Tuesday works" matter more than new sellers realize.

**Stop selling.** This is the big one. The yes is in hand. Every extra sentence of pitch after the yes can only lose it. Confirm, thank, get off the phone.

**The wrap-up, in order:**

1. Confirm the details out loud (day, time, who calls whom or where).
2. Tell them exactly what to expect: "I'll call you, 15 minutes, I'll show you what I found on your online presence, and you decide if it's worth anything. Nothing to prepare."
3. Send the calendar invite and log the call in your CRM or sheet immediately: who, when, what you promised, anything personal they mentioned (kid's baseball, busy with a big commercial job). You'll open the meeting with it.
4. Do your homework before the meeting. If you promised to pull their rankings or look at their site, actually do it. The fastest way to lose a cold-booked meeting is to show up with nothing.

Then dial the next number. A booked meeting is a great feeling. Don't spend twenty minutes enjoying it.
