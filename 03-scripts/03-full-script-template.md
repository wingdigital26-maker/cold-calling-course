# The Full Call: Hello to Booked Meeting

This is the whole call, annotated. Lines you say are in quotes. Notes on WHY each line works are underneath in italics.

Fill in every placeholder before you dial. Keep this in front of you, but treat it as a map, not a teleprompter. If the conversation goes somewhere good that isn't on the page, follow the conversation.

The goal of this call is NOT to sell your service. It is to book a 15-20 minute meeting. That's the only yes you need.

---

## Stage 1: The open (0:00 to 0:15)

**Them:** "Hello?" / "[COMPANY], this is [NAME]."

**You:**
> "Hey [NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] with [YOUR COMPANY]. I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call. Do you want to hang up, or can I take 30 seconds to tell you why I called?"

*Why it works: You named the elephant. They were already 90% sure this was a sales call, and confirming it yourself makes you the rare honest one. Offering the hang-up makes saying yes feel like their choice, and people defend their own choices. Deliver it slow and a little amused at yourself, not apologetic.*

**If they answer but you're not sure it's the owner:**
> "Quick check before I waste your time, am I talking to the owner, or is that someone else?"

*Why: Pitching a crew member burns the call. Asking "or is that someone else" gives them an easy way to redirect you without feeling interrogated.*

---

## Stage 2: The reason for the call (0:15 to 0:45)

They said "go ahead" or "make it quick." Now spend the 30 seconds you asked for. This is your value statement (see [02-value-statements.md](02-value-statements.md)).

**You:**
> "Appreciate it. So, I looked at [COMPANY] before I called. When somebody in [CITY] googles '[TRADE] near me,' [COMPETITOR] shows up ahead of you, which means they're getting calls that could be yours. That's the thing I fix for [TRADE] companies. I don't know yet if it makes sense for you, that's why I'm calling."

*Why it works: It's specific (their city, a real competitor), it's a problem they can verify themselves, and "I don't know yet if it makes sense for you" removes the pushy vibe. You're qualifying them, not begging.*

Then ask ONE question and shut up:

> "Can I ask, where does most of your work come from right now?"

*Why: The fastest way to stop sounding like a salesman is to start sounding like someone curious about their business. Their answer tells you which branch you're in and gives you ammunition for the close. Whatever they say, actually listen. See [04-discovery-questions.md](04-discovery-questions.md).*

---

## Stage 3: The branches

Everything up to here is one script. Now it forks based on their temperature.

### Branch A: Interested ("Yeah, we could use more calls" / asks you questions)

Do NOT start pitching features. Interest means it's time to book, not time to talk more.

**You:**
> "Okay, that's what I figured, and honestly this isn't a thing I can do justice on a cold call. Here's what I'd suggest: give me 15 minutes this week. I'll pull up your website and your Google listing before we talk, show you exactly what I'm seeing, and what I'd do about it. If it's not a fit, you got a free look at your own business and you never hear from me again. Does [DAY] morning or [DAY] afternoon work better?"

*Why it works: "Can't do it justice on a cold call" is true and it respects their time. Promising to do homework before the meeting gives the meeting standalone value. "If it's not a fit, you never hear from me again" lowers the risk of saying yes. And offering two times instead of asking "when are you free" makes booking a small choice instead of an open question.*

**Then lock it down:**
> "Perfect. What's the best email for you? I'll send a calendar invite right now so it's on both our calendars... Alright, [DAY] at [TIME]. One thing, I'll only need you, no pressure to have anyone else on. If something blows up and you can't make it, just shoot me a text at this number instead of ghosting me, deal?"

*Why: The invite makes it real. The "text me instead of ghosting" line is playful but drastically cuts no-shows, because now flaking on you silently feels like breaking a small promise.*

### Branch B: Hesitant ("I don't know..." / "We already work with somebody" / "Money's tight" / "Send me some info")

Hesitation is not a no. It's fear of a sales pitch. Your job is to lower the stakes, not to push harder.

**"We already work with somebody":**
> "That's fair, and I'm not going to trash them, if it's working, keep it. Let me ask you this though: are you actually seeing more calls come in, or are you kind of taking it on faith? ... If you're even a little unsure, that's exactly what the 15 minutes is for. I'll show you where you stand right now, in plain numbers. Worst case you go back to your current guy with better questions."

*Why: Refusing to trash the competitor builds trust instantly. The question "or are you taking it on faith" lands because most owners genuinely don't know what their marketing does. And "go back to your current guy with better questions" makes the meeting safe, it doesn't require leaving anyone.*

**"Money's tight" / "Not in the budget":**
> "Totally get it, and I'm not asking you to spend anything today. The 15-minute call costs you nothing, and if the math doesn't obviously work, I'll tell you that myself. I'd rather tell you no than take money that doesn't come back to you."

*Why: You separated the meeting from the money. "I'd rather tell you no" is disarming because salespeople never say it. Only say it if you mean it.*

**"Just send me some info":**
> "I can do that, but let me be honest, that email's going to die in your inbox and we both know it. How about this instead: 15 minutes, I show you what I'd send you anyway, but on a screen where you can ask questions. If it's junk, delete me like the email. [DAY] or [DAY]?"

*Why: "Send me info" is usually a polite brush-off. Naming that, with humor, either converts it into a real meeting or surfaces the real objection so you can deal with it. If they insist on the email, send a SHORT one and calendar a follow-up call for three days later.*

**Hesitant but warming ("I mean... maybe, what would you even do?"):**
> "Good question, and the honest answer is I don't fully know yet, because I'd want to look under the hood first. That's the point of the 15 minutes. But broadly: [ONE SENTENCE, e.g. 'get you showing up first when people in [CITY] search, and make the phone ring more']. Fair enough?"

*Why: Refusing to give a canned answer proves you're not running a script on rails. One plain sentence satisfies the question without turning the call into a pitch.*

### Branch C: Busy ("I'm on a roof right now" / "In the middle of something")

Believe them. Trades people usually ARE busy. Do not pitch a man on a ladder.

**You:**
> "Say no more, I'll be quick. I help [TRADE] companies in [CITY] get more calls. That's the whole pitch. When's a better time to catch you for two real minutes, end of day or early morning?"

*Why: Respecting their time IS the pitch. You compressed everything to one sentence and asked a specific scheduling question. "End of day or early morning" fits how trades actually work: before the crew rolls out, or after.*

**Then:**
> "Alright, I'll call you [DAY] at [TIME]. I'll actually call, so if you'd rather I didn't, tell me now and I'll get lost. Otherwise talk then."

*Why: "I'll actually call" sets up the callback as a kept promise, which starts the relationship on reliability. And the exit offer filters out people who were just brushing you off, saving you a wasted callback.*

### Branch D: Hard no ("Not interested, don't call again")

**You:**
> "No problem, [NAME]. Taking you off my list. Good luck out there."

*Why: Say it, mean it, hang up, and actually take them off the list. Arguing with a hard no burns time and reputation. A graceful exit sometimes even gets a "wait, what was it about?" Either way, next call.*

---

## Stage 4: After the call (2 minutes, every call)

- Log the outcome: booked / callback scheduled / not interested / no answer.
- Write ONE line about them: "sounded slammed, does mostly insurance work, competitor mentioned was [X]." Future-you needs this on the follow-up.
- If booked: send the calendar invite NOW, plus a one-line text: "Good talking, [NAME]. Invite's in your email for [DAY] at [TIME]. - [YOUR NAME]"
- If callback: put it in your calendar with the note. A promised callback you forget is worse than never calling.

---

## The shape of the whole thing

1. Honest open, earn 30 seconds.
2. Specific value statement, one discovery question.
3. Branch: interested → book, hesitant → lower stakes → book, busy → schedule callback, no → exit clean.
4. Book the meeting, lock it with invite + text.

Every line in this script exists to get one small yes. Never try to skip to the big one.
