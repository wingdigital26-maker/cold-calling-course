# Openers: The First 10 Seconds

## Why the first 10 seconds decide the call

When a business owner picks up an unknown number, their brain is doing one thing: sorting you. Customer, vendor, or telemarketer. That sort happens fast, usually before you finish your second sentence. Once you get filed under "telemarketer," everything you say afterward gets heard through that filter, and most people hang up or brush you off out of habit, not because of your offer.

So the opener has one job. It is not to sell. It is not to pitch. It is to earn the next 30 seconds. That's it.

Three things kill you in the first 10 seconds:

1. **Sounding like every other cold caller.** "Hi, how are you today?!" in a chipper voice is a telemarketer's uniform. The moment they hear it, you're sorted.
2. **Talking fast.** Nervous callers rush. Rushing signals "I know you want to hang up." Slow down. Calm is credible.
3. **Hiding the fact that it's a cold call.** They already know. Pretending otherwise makes you sound sneaky, and sneaky gets hung up on.

The openers below all solve the same problem in different ways: they break the telemarketer pattern, they respect the prospect's time, and they buy you permission to keep talking.

One rule for all of them: after you deliver the opener, **stop talking**. Let them respond. The silence feels brutal at first. Use it anyway.

---

## 1. Permission-based opener

**Line:**
> "Hey [NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] with [YOUR COMPANY]. I'll be honest, this is a cold call. Do you want to hang up, or can I have 30 seconds to tell you why I called?"

**When to use it:** Your default. Works on almost everyone because it does the opposite of what they expect. You name the thing they're already thinking, and you hand them control. Most people, given the choice, will give you the 30 seconds. And now they've *agreed* to listen, which changes the whole dynamic.

**Watch for:** If they say "go ahead," your next sentence better be tight. You asked for 30 seconds. Use 15.

---

## 2. Pattern interrupt

**Line:**
> "Hey [NAME], you're probably going to hate me for this... this is a cold call. Want to give me 30 seconds, or should I get lost?"

**When to use it:** With owners of blue-collar businesses (roofing, plumbing, landscaping) who get hammered by polished salespeople all day. The self-deprecating humor breaks the script in their head. They usually laugh or at least soften. Don't use it with someone who sounds rushed or annoyed from the first "hello," because humor lands badly on a stressed person.

---

## 3. Honest and direct

**Line:**
> "Hi [NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] from [YOUR COMPANY]. This is a sales call. I work with [TRADE] companies in [CITY], and I think I can get you more jobs. Have I caught you at a terrible time?"

**When to use it:** When you want zero games. Good for older, no-nonsense owners who respect straight shooters. "Have I caught you at a terrible time" beats "is now a good time" because it's honest (it probably IS a bad time) and it gives them an easy out that most won't take. People soften when you assume the worst instead of pretending everything is convenient.

---

## 4. Referral-adjacent (the "I was looking at your area" opener)

**Line:**
> "Hey [NAME], I was just working with another [TRADE] company over in [NEARBY CITY], and while I was looking at the market I came across [COMPANY]. Figured I'd just call you directly. You the owner?"

**When to use it:** Only when it's TRUE. If you actually have a client or an active conversation in a nearby city, this is powerful because it makes the call feel specific, not sprayed. Never invent the other company. If they ask "who?", you need a real answer, and if the client hasn't agreed to be named, say "I keep client names private, same as I would for you, but they're a [TRADE] company about 20 minutes from you."

---

## 5. Local angle

**Line:**
> "Hey [NAME], this is [YOUR NAME]. I'm calling [TRADE] companies around [CITY] specifically. Quick question: when someone in [CITY] searches for a [TRADE], are you happy with where you show up?"

**When to use it:** When your service ties to local visibility (websites, SEO, ads, review management). Naming their city twice makes the call feel researched. The question at the end forces a real answer, and almost nobody says "yes, completely happy," which opens the door.

---

## 6. The "I did my homework" opener

**Line:**
> "Hey [NAME], this is [YOUR NAME]. Before I called I actually looked at [COMPANY]'s website and your Google listing. I noticed a couple things I'd want to know about if it were my business. Got a minute, or is this a bad time?"

**When to use it:** When you genuinely spent two minutes looking them up and found something specific (site doesn't load on mobile, 4 reviews when a competitor has 90, no website at all). Specificity is the whole play here. If they say "like what?", tell them exactly what you saw, plainly, without piling on. You're a person who noticed something, not a critic.

---

## 7. The single-question opener

**Line:**
> "Hey [NAME], [YOUR NAME] here. I'll keep this stupid short. One question: are you taking on new [ROOFING/HVAC/PLUMBING] jobs right now, or are you booked out?"

**When to use it:** With trades in busy season, or when you keep getting cut off before finishing longer openers. It's disarming because it sounds like a customer question, and every answer works for you. "We're booked" leads to "good problem, so the goal would be keeping it that way in the slow months." "We could take more" leads straight to your value statement.

---

## 8. The straight-to-the-point opener (no permission, no games)

**Line:**
> "[NAME]? [YOUR NAME] with [YOUR COMPANY]. The reason I'm calling: I help [TRADE] companies in [CITY] get more calls from people who need [SERVICE]. I don't know yet if I can help you, that's why I'm calling. Fair enough if I ask you two quick questions?"

**When to use it:** When you've got reps under your belt and can deliver it calmly. "I don't know yet if I can help you" is the key phrase. It kills the pushy-salesman vibe because you're not claiming a miracle, you're qualifying them. Confident, not desperate.

---

## 9. The callback-style opener

**Line:**
> "Hey [NAME], it's [YOUR NAME] over at [YOUR COMPANY]. I tried you [LAST WEEK/A FEW DAYS AGO] and didn't want to be that guy who just keeps calling, so I figured I'd try once more and then leave you alone. Got 30 seconds?"

**When to use it:** Second or third attempt only, and only if you actually called before. It works because it acknowledges the annoyance of repeat calls before they can feel it, and "then leave you alone" lowers the stakes. More on multi-attempt calling in [05-voicemail-and-callbacks.md](05-voicemail-and-callbacks.md).

---

## Picking yours

Don't rotate all nine. Pick one primary (start with the permission-based opener, it's the most forgiving) and one backup for when you can hear the prospect is rushed (the single-question opener is good for that). Run your primary for at least 50 calls before judging it. The opener is rarely the problem. Delivery is. Slow, calm, and lower-pitched than your nervous voice wants to be.
