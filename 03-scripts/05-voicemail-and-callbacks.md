# Voicemail and Callbacks

Most of your dials will not be answered. That's not failure, that's the job. This file covers what to do with the no-answers: when to leave voicemail, what to say in it, and how to run second and third attempts so they build on each other instead of just repeating attempt one.

## Should you leave a voicemail?

Short answer: not on the first attempt, yes on the second.

- **First attempt, no answer:** hang up, log it, move on. You want a clean second attempt where the phone rings without "that guy who already left a message" attached to it. Business owners rarely call sales voicemails back, so the message's job is not a callback anyway.
- **Second attempt, no answer:** leave the voicemail. By now you know a live pickup is not guaranteed, so make yourself familiar instead. The real purpose of the voicemail is to warm up attempt three: when your number shows up again, you're a name, not a stranger.
- **Don't expect callbacks.** If your voicemail gets you a few callbacks ever, celebrate them, but plan as if it gets zero. It's a seed, not a harvest.

One exception: if your research found something specific and genuinely useful (their site is down, their Google listing shows them permanently closed), leave that voicemail on attempt one. Useful beats familiar.

## The 15-20 second voicemail script

Time yourself. Past 20 seconds, people skip.

> "Hey [NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] with [YOUR COMPANY]. I work with [TRADE] companies around [CITY] and I noticed something about how [COMPANY] shows up when people search for a [TRADE] there. Nothing's on fire, but it's probably costing you calls. I'll try you again [DAY], or you can catch me at [YOUR NUMBER]. Again, that's [YOUR NAME], [YOUR NUMBER]. Talk soon."

Why each piece is there:

- **"I noticed something about [COMPANY]"** creates a specific open loop. A vague "I'd love to chat about your marketing" closes itself, deleted. A specific unanswered "noticed something" itches.
- **"Nothing's on fire"** keeps it honest. Fake-urgency voicemails ("it's important that you call me back") are a scam pattern and owners know it.
- **"I'll try you again [DAY]"** is the actual point. You're not requesting a callback, you're announcing the next attempt, so when it comes, it's expected instead of annoying.
- **Number twice, slowly.** Nobody replays a voicemail to catch a mumbled number. Say it at half the speed that feels natural.

Slow down overall. A rushed voicemail sounds like every other rushed sales voicemail.

## Running attempts two and three differently

The worst multi-attempt strategy is the same call three times. Each attempt should be different in when it comes and what it says.

**Spacing:** attempt 1, then attempt 2 about 2-3 days later, then attempt 3 about a week after that. Tight enough to stay familiar, loose enough not to feel stalked.

**Vary the time of day.** This alone fixes most no-answer streaks. If attempt one was 10am, try 8am next (before the crew rolls out) or 4:30pm (back in the truck). Trades owners have rhythms; your job is finding the gap in theirs.

### Attempt 2 (they didn't answer attempt 1, or brushed you off)

If they pick up, acknowledge the history, don't pretend it's fresh:

> "Hey [NAME], it's [YOUR NAME] with [YOUR COMPANY]. I tried you earlier this week, didn't want to be a pest so I figured one more shot. Got 30 seconds?"

Why: naming the repeat call defuses the "ugh, this guy again" before it forms. "One more shot" signals you're not an infinite robo-dialer.

If no answer: leave the voicemail above.

### Attempt 3 (final attempt)

If they pick up, use the takeaway. Genuinely low pressure, and paradoxically your best-converting call:

> "Hey [NAME], [YOUR NAME] again. Third time's the limit, so this is the last one, promise. Before I close your file, I figured I'd ask straight: is 'more calls from [CITY] homeowners' something you care about this year, or should I quit bugging you? Either answer's fine."

Why it works: "last one, promise" removes the fear that engaging means endless calls. "Before I close your file" is a mild takeaway, and people react to doors closing. "Either answer's fine" makes the no easy, which weirdly makes the yes easier too. You'll get real answers here that attempts one and two never got, including "actually, what is it you do?"

If no answer on attempt 3, one last short voicemail:

> "Hey [NAME], [YOUR NAME] with [YOUR COMPANY]. Third time I've tried you so I'll take the hint and leave you alone. If more [TRADE] jobs in [CITY] ever becomes a priority, I'm at [YOUR NUMBER]. That's [YOUR NUMBER]. Good luck out there."

Then actually stop. Move them to a long-term list and maybe try again next season. A graceful exit keeps the door open in a way a seventh unanswered call never will.

## Between attempts: the text follow-up

If you have their cell (many trades numbers are cells), one short text after the attempt-2 voicemail can outperform every voicemail you ever leave, because texts get read:

> "Hey [NAME], [YOUR NAME] here, just left you a voicemail. I help [TRADE] companies in [CITY] get more calls. No pressure at all, happy to text if that's easier."

Keep it to one text. Two unanswered texts is a message; three is a nuisance. And "happy to text if that's easier" matters, because plenty of owners will type what they'd never say to a salesperson on the phone.

## Track it or lose it

None of this works from memory. For every prospect log: attempt number, date, time of day, outcome, and voicemail yes/no. A spreadsheet is fine. The system is what makes attempt three feel like a relationship instead of a rerun, and it's also what stops you calling someone a fourth time after you promised you wouldn't.
