# Discovery Questions

Discovery is the part of the call where you stop pitching and start learning. It matters for two reasons: their answers tell you whether this prospect is worth your time, and the act of being asked good questions is what makes YOU feel different from every other caller. Nobody else asks. Everybody else pitches.

On a cold call you'll only get to ask 2 or 3 of these. Save the rest for the booked meeting. Pick the ones that fit where the conversation already is.

## The listening ratio

Aim for the prospect talking about twice as much as you during discovery. If you catch yourself explaining, teaching, or pitching between questions, stop. Ask, then be quiet, all the way through the awkward pause. People fill silence with the truth.

And this is a conversation, not an interrogation. The pattern is: ask, listen, REACT to what they said, then ask the next thing. "Huh, mostly insurance work? That's interesting, so when there's no storm, what happens?" flows. Firing questions off a checklist ("Question two: what is your budget?") feels like a survey and gets you hung up on. If their answer opens a door the list doesn't cover, walk through the door.

## Situation questions (find out how the business actually runs)

Use these early. They're easy to answer and get the prospect talking.

1. "Where does most of your work come from right now?"
   The single best opening question. Word of mouth, referrals, storms, a lead service, their answer frames the whole conversation.

2. "Is it just you running things, or do you have somebody handling the office side?"
   Tells you who really makes decisions and who you'd be working with.

3. "How many crews are you running these days?"
   Size check, in their language. A two-truck shop and a fifteen-crew operation need different pitches.

4. "Are you trying to grow right now, or more trying to keep the machine running?"
   Massive filter. A growth-mode owner hears your pitch completely differently than one who's maxed out.

5. "Have you ever paid anyone for marketing before? How'd that go?"
   Most trades owners have been burned. If they have, you need to know now, because that burn is the real objection you'll face later.

## Pain questions (find where it hurts)

Only ask these after a situation question or two, once they're talking.

6. "When the phone's slow, what do you do? Just ride it out?"
   Surfaces the feast-or-famine pain gently. "Ride it out" said with a sigh is a buying signal.

7. "When someone in [CITY] needs a [TRADE] and doesn't know anybody, who do you think they end up calling?"
   Makes them confront the invisible-online problem themselves, which lands harder than you telling them.

8. "How many calls a week would you say come in from people who found you online, not referrals?"
   Most owners don't know, and realizing they don't know is the pain.

9. "Are the jobs you're getting the ones you actually want, or do you take a lot of small stuff to stay busy?"
   Opens the quality-of-work pain. Many trades don't need more jobs, they need better ones.

10. "What happened the last time a storm came through? Did you get your share of that work?"
    (Roofing specific, adapt per trade.) Ties pain to a concrete recent memory instead of an abstraction.

11. "If the phone stopped ringing for two weeks, how bad would that be?"
    Blunt, so use it only when the conversation is already honest. It measures how thin the margin really is.

## Priority questions (find out if they'd actually act)

These separate a pleasant chat from a real opportunity. Ask near the end, or in the booked meeting.

12. "Out of everything we just talked about, what's the one that actually bugs you?"
    Lets THEM rank the pain. Whatever they name is the only thing your follow-up should be about.

13. "Have you tried to fix that before, or has it just been sitting on the someday list?"
    Tells you whether this pain has ever converted into action. "Someday list" people need a smaller first step.

14. "If we could fix that, what would it be worth to you? Like what does one good [ROOF/INSTALL/CONTRACT] job pay?"
    Gets THEM to do the math on value, using their own numbers. Never supply the numbers yourself.

15. "If I show you something that makes sense on [MEETING DAY], is there anything that would stop you from moving on it? Anyone else who'd need to weigh in?"
    The pre-close. Surfaces the spouse, the business partner, or the "not until spring" timeline BEFORE you spend an hour on a proposal.

---

## Three rules

- **Two or three per cold call, max.** Discovery on a cold call is seasoning, not the meal. The meal is the booked meeting.
- **Write down their answers.** Verbatim phrases especially. Opening the booked meeting with "you said the thing that bugs you is losing storm work to out-of-town crews" proves you listened, and proving you listened is half the sale.
- **Never ask a question and then ignore the answer.** One robotic "great!" followed by an unrelated pitch undoes everything. If you're not going to use the answer, don't ask the question.
