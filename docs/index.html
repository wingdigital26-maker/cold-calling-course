Module 1

The mindset

Cold calling is a numbers game played with a good attitude. Rejection is not failure. It is the cost of finding the people who need us.

  • 1Most dials don't even connect. Of the ones that do, most say no. That is normal for everyone, including the best callers alive.
  • 2Rough ballpark: a beginner books one meeting per 100 to 300 dials. It gets much better with reps. Do not judge yourself on any single call.
  • 3Your energy is audible. Stand up, smile while you talk, keep a steady pace. The prospect hears your posture.
  • 4Detach from the outcome of each call. Attach to the number of dials. Dials are the only thing you fully control.

Module 1

Anatomy of a cold call

Every good cold call has the same five beats. The first ten seconds decide whether you get the rest.

1. Opener

Say who you are, honestly, and ask for a moment. No tricks, no pretending you've talked before.

2. Reason for the call

One specific thing you noticed about THEIR business. This is why research matters.

3. Value in one sentence

The problem they feel, the outcome we deliver. Not a lecture about SEO.

4. One good question

Get them talking about their situation. Listen twice as much as you talk.

5. Ask for the meeting

Small ask: 15 minutes with Jack. Offer two concrete times. That's the whole goal.

Module 3

The script, condensed

"Hi [NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] with [YOUR COMPANY]. I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call, but I promise it's a quick one. Have you got 30 seconds?"

Then the reason: something real you saw. Their review count. Their site on a phone. How they rank in their own city.

The 30-second pitch

"We're a marketing company that works with [TRADE] businesses. When someone in [CITY] searches for what you do, we make sure you show up, look better than the other guys, and get the call. I can't tell you what you need over the phone. That's the 15 minutes with Jack."

The voicemail (under 20 seconds)

"Hey [NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] with [YOUR COMPANY]. I noticed something about how [COMPANY] shows up when people search for a [TRADE]. Nothing's on fire, but it's costing you calls. I'll try you again [DAY], or catch me at [YOUR NUMBER]. Talk soon."

Gatekeepers

Be pleasant and direct: "Hi, this is [YOUR NAME] from [YOUR COMPANY]. Is the owner around? It's about the company's website." Never lie to a gatekeeper. Never pretend to be someone you're not.

Module 4

Objections are reflexes, not answers

The pattern for every objection: acknowledge it, clarify what's behind it, reframe once, ask again. One loop, maybe two. Never three.

"Not interested"

Usually means "I don't want to be sold right now."

"Totally fair, I called out of nowhere. Can I ask one thing before I let you go: when someone searches [TRADE] in [CITY], do you know where you show up?"

"Send me an email"

Usually a polite brush-off.

"Happy to. So I send something useful instead of junk: is the website or getting found on Google the bigger headache right now?"

"How much is it?"

Real interest, wrong moment.

"Honest answer: it depends on what your site and your Google presence actually need. That's literally what the 15 minutes is for. Might be less than you think."

"Too busy"

Probably true. They're on a roof.

"That's exactly why I'll be quick. Busy season is the best time to fix what brings the next season's jobs. Fifteen minutes, [DAY] or [DAY]?"

All ten common objections, each with two full word-for-word responses, are in Module 4 of the written course.

Module 4

Booking the meeting

  • 1Keep the ask small. Fifteen minutes, that's it.
  • 2Offer two options, not an open question: "Tuesday morning or Wednesday afternoon?"
  • 3Send the calendar invite before you dial the next number. Not at the end of the day. Now.
  • 4Confirm same-day with a quick text or email. This is the biggest no-show killer we have.
  • 5A real no is a real no. End gracefully, leave the door open, move on. Burned bridges cost referrals.
Hard rules

The honesty rules

These are not suggestions. Breaking them gets meetings that fall apart and a reputation that follows us around town.

Never

  • Quote a price. Not a number, not a range, not "probably around".
  • Promise rankings or lead counts.
  • Fake knowledge. "Good question, Jack covers that on the call."
  • Claim a referral, a callback, or a past conversation that didn't happen.
  • Trash their current setup beyond what you actually saw.
  • Call anyone who asked not to be called.

Always

  • Say who you are and where you're calling from.
  • Respect calling hours (8am to 9pm their time, and business hours in practice).
  • Log every call outcome on the call sheet.
  • Sell the 15-minute conversation, not the service.
  • Let them talk twice as much as you do.
Module 5

Your first 30 days

Week 1: Learn and drill

Read the full course. Read the scripts out loud daily. Roleplay with an AI chatbot using the prompts in Module 5. Zero real dials yet.

Week 2: First 50 dials

Ten dials a day on real prospects. The goal is reps and comfort, not bookings. Log everything.

Week 3: Volume

Twenty dials a day. Weekly review of your numbers. Do not touch the script yet; you don't have enough data.

Week 4: Refine and settle

Twenty-five a day, then design your permanent daily block: 60 to 90 minutes, 20 to 30 dials, every working day.

Last step

Now: your questions

You just took in a lot. Before we talk, write down every question you have, big or small. Especially the ones that feel dumb. Those are usually the important ones.

Good questions to bring

Anything about what to say, what we sell, what a specific objection means, or what happens after a meeting gets booked.

Don't worry about

How the websites, SEO, or systems are actually built and run. That gets taught in person, hands-on, after you're comfortable on the phone.
