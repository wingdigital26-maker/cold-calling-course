# Reviewing Your Own Calls

Nobody improves at cold calling from memory. In the moment you are too busy surviving the call to notice what you are doing. Recordings do not lie.

## First: recording legality

Call recording laws vary by state and country. Some places require both parties to consent. Before recording any real call, check the rules where you and your prospects are located. If in doubt, review from memory using the same scorecard immediately after the call. It is weaker but still useful.

## What to review

Do not review every call. Rough guidance: pick 2 or 3 calls per day, and make sure at least one of them went badly. Reviewing only your good calls feels nice and teaches nothing.

## What to listen for

### 1. Talk ratio

Who talked more, you or them? On a cold call you will naturally talk more at the open, but if the whole call is you delivering a monologue, you were pitching at them, not talking with them. Listen for whether you asked questions and then actually stopped talking.

Warning signs:

- You answer your own questions before the prospect can.
- The prospect's longest response is three words.
- You cannot remember a single fact about their business after the call.

### 2. Filler words and verbal leaks

Count your "um", "uh", "like", "you know", "just", and "sort of". You do not need zero. You need to hear how many you actually use, because it is always more than you think. "Just" is the sneaky one: "I was just calling to..." shrinks you before the pitch even starts.

Also listen for nervous laughter and apologizing ("sorry to bother you"). Both signal that you think the call is an imposition, and the prospect will agree with you.

### 3. Did you actually ask for the meeting?

This is the most common failure and the easiest to miss from memory. Many calls that felt fine end with something mushy like "so yeah, feel free to check out our website." Listen for the moment you asked for a specific meeting at a specific time. If it never happened, the call was a conversation, not a cold call.

Also listen to how you asked. Did your voice go up at the end like you were bracing for a no? Was it buried in a run-on sentence? A clean ask sounds like: "Let's grab 15 minutes. Does Tuesday morning work?"

### 4. The first 15 seconds

Replay the open twice. Most calls are won or lost there. Did you sound like every other sales call they got that week, or like a person with a reason to call them specifically?

## The self-scorecard

Score every reviewed call 1 to 5 on each row. 1 means it did not happen or went badly, 5 means you would show this call to someone as an example. Be harsh. Inflated scores waste your own time.

| # | Category | What a 5 looks like | Score (1-5) |
|---|----------|---------------------|-------------|
| 1 | Opener | Confident, specific reason for calling, earned the next 30 seconds | |
| 2 | Tone | Calm, smiling, unhurried, downward inflection on key lines | |
| 3 | Talk ratio | Prospect talked meaningfully; I asked and then listened | |
| 4 | Filler words | Few enough that I sound deliberate, no apologizing | |
| 5 | Objection handling | Acknowledged first, responded calmly, did not argue | |
| 6 | The ask | Clearly asked for a specific meeting time, then shut up | |
| 7 | Next step | Call ended with a concrete outcome, even if that outcome was a clean no | |

## How to use the scores

- Track your total per reviewed call in your tracking sheet (see the next file).
- Do not chase the total. Chase your lowest recurring row. If "the ask" scores 1 or 2 three reviews in a row, that is your one focus for the next call block. Fix one thing at a time.
- Re-listen to a call from two weeks ago once in a while. Hearing your own progress is the best motivation in this entire course.
