# Roleplay With an AI Assistant

An AI chatbot is a free, infinitely patient roleplay partner that never gets bored and never judges you. Use it to take your first punches before a real prospect throws them.

These prompts work in any AI assistant. Paste one in, then respond as if you were on a live call. Type what you would actually say out loud. Better yet, say it out loud first, then type it.

## How to get the most out of it

- Do not edit your responses. Type your first instinct. On a real call there is no backspace.
- Play the whole call out, even when it goes badly. Bad reps teach more than good ones.
- Run each scenario multiple times. The AI will vary its responses, and you will handle them better each round.
- After each roleplay, ask for the scorecard (prompt at the bottom).

## Prompt 1: The easy prospect

Use this first. It builds the muscle of running your script start to finish with a cooperative partner.

```
I want to practice cold calling. You are the owner of a small local plumbing
company. I am calling to offer marketing services. Play a POLITE and mildly
interested prospect: you answer the phone normally, you ask reasonable
questions like "what does this cost" and "how is this different from other
agencies", and if I handle the call reasonably well you agree to a short
meeting. Do not make it too easy: ask at least two real questions before
saying yes. Stay in character the entire time. Only respond with what the
prospect would say, nothing else. Start the roleplay now by answering the
phone.
```

## Prompt 2: The tough prospect

Use this once the easy version feels comfortable. This is where the real training happens.

```
I want to practice cold calling. You are the busy owner of a local roofing
company. I am calling to offer marketing services. Play a TOUGH but realistic
prospect: you are skeptical, short on time, and slightly annoyed at getting
a sales call. Open with resistance like "I'm busy, what is this?" Use common
objections during the call: "not interested", "we already have someone",
"just send me an email", "how much is it?" Do not be cartoonishly rude, and
do not be impossible: if I handle your objections well and give you a real
reason to care, you can gradually warm up. If I do badly, end the call the
way a real busy owner would. Stay in character the entire time. Only respond
with what the prospect would say. Start the roleplay now by answering the
phone.
```

## Prompt 3: The gatekeeper

Most calls to local businesses do not reach the owner first. Drill getting past the front desk.

```
I want to practice cold calling. You are the receptionist at a local HVAC
company. I am calling to reach the owner. Play a realistic gatekeeper: your
job is to screen sales calls. Ask questions like "who's calling?", "what is
this regarding?", and "is he expecting your call?" If I sound like a generic
salesperson, tell me the owner is unavailable and offer to take a message.
If I am direct, confident, and treat you like a person rather than an
obstacle, you can either put me through or give me useful information like
the owner's name or a better time to call. Stay in character the entire
time. Only respond with what the receptionist would say. Start the roleplay
now by answering the phone.
```

## Ask for a scorecard after every roleplay

When the roleplay ends, paste this in the same chat:

```
The roleplay is over. Break character. Score my performance on the call
from 1 to 5 on each of the following, with one specific example from the
call for each score:

1. Opener: did I earn the right to keep talking in the first 15 seconds?
2. Tone and confidence: did I sound like a peer or like a nervous seller?
3. Listening: did I respond to what you actually said, or just push my script?
4. Objection handling: did I acknowledge before responding, or argue?
5. The ask: did I clearly ask for a specific meeting time?

Then tell me the single biggest thing to fix before my next call, and
rewrite one line I said as a stronger version.
```

## A simple weekly routine

Rough guidance, adjust to taste:

- 2 easy-prospect roleplays when a script is new.
- 3 to 5 tough-prospect roleplays per week, every week.
- 2 gatekeeper roleplays per week if you call businesses with front desks.
- Always end with the scorecard. Keep a note of the "biggest thing to fix" from each session and watch for repeats. A repeated note is your real weakness.

One warning: AI roleplay is practice, not proof. The AI is more patient and more logical than real people. Treat it like a batting cage, not a game.
