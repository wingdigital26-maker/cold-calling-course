# Tracking and KPIs

You cannot fix what you do not measure, and in cold calling almost everyone measures nothing. Four numbers, tracked daily, will tell you more than any guru.

## The four numbers

| Metric | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| Dials | Every time you press call, including no-answers and voicemails |
| Connects | A human answered, any human, even the gatekeeper |
| Conversations | You reached the decision maker and got past your opener into a real exchange |
| Meetings | A specific meeting was booked with a date and time |

Definitions matter. "They said call back next week" is not a meeting. "The receptionist answered and hung up" is still a connect. Be strict or the data is worthless.

Connect rate = connects / dials. Meetings per 100 dials = (meetings / dials) x 100. These two ratios are your dashboard.

There are no universal "good" numbers. Rates vary wildly by industry, list quality, and time of day. Rough guidance only: many callers to local businesses see somewhere in the range of 15 to 30 percent of dials connect, and early on most people book few or zero meetings per 100 dials. Your baseline is your own week 2 numbers. Beat those.

## Weekly review questions

Sit down once a week, look at the rollup, and answer these in writing:

1. Did I hit my dial target? If not, why actually? (Be honest: "I avoided the phone" is a common and fixable answer.)
2. Is my connect rate moving? Low connect rate is usually a list or timing problem, not a skill problem. What times of day connected best?
3. Am I converting connects into real conversations? If gatekeepers or openers are killing me, that is the drill for next week.
4. Am I asking for the meeting on every conversation? Check your call reviews. A conversation without an ask does not count as a real attempt.
5. What is the one thing I will change next week? One. Not five.

## When to change the script vs when to just dial more

This is where most beginners sabotage themselves. A bad streak feels like a script problem. It usually is not.

**Sample size caution: do not rewrite your script after 20 dials.** Rough guidance: 20 dials might contain only 4 to 6 connects and 1 or 2 real conversations. That is a coin flip's worth of data. You could run the best script on earth and still have a miserable 20-dial stretch.

Use this decision guide:

| Situation | Diagnosis | Action |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Under ~100 dials on the current script | Not enough data | Just dial more. Change nothing. |
| Low connect rate, decent results when you do connect | List or timing problem | Better list, different call times. Script is fine. |
| Connects fine, but conversations die in the first 15 seconds, repeatedly, across 100+ dials | Opener problem | Change the opener only. Keep everything else. |
| Conversations go fine but no meetings, and reviews show you ARE asking | Offer or ask problem | Adjust the ask or the offer framing. One change. |
| Conversations go fine but reviews show you are NOT asking | You, not the script | Drill the ask. Do not touch the script. |

Two rules:

- **Change one variable at a time.** If you change the opener, the offer, and the list in the same week, you learn nothing no matter what happens.
- **Give every change a fair test.** Rough guidance: at least 50 to 100 dials on the new version before judging it.

Most of the time, the answer to "what should I change?" is nothing. Dial more.
