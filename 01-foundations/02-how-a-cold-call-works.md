# Anatomy of a Cold Call

A cold call is not a conversation that wanders wherever it wants. A good one has a structure: five stages, in order, each with one job. When a call goes sideways, it is almost always because one stage got skipped or botched.

The five stages:

1. Opener
2. Reason for the call
3. Value statement
4. Discovery question
5. Ask for the meeting

Total time if it goes well: 2 to 5 minutes. Here is each stage, what its job is, and what good sounds like.

## 1. The opener

**Job:** survive the first ten seconds. Nothing else. You are not selling anything yet. You are earning the right to say one more sentence.

The prospect's brain in the first seconds is running one question: "Is this a telemarketer I can hang up on?" Your opener has to interrupt that pattern.

What good sounds like:

> "Hey, is this Mike? ... Mike, this is Jack. I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call. You can hang up, or give me 30 seconds and I'll tell you why I called. Fair?"

Why it works: it is honest, it gives them control, and it does not sound like a script. Most people, given the choice, let you have the 30 seconds.

Another style, more casual:

> "Hey Mike, Jack here. We haven't spoken before. Did I catch you in the middle of something?"

Yes, you are inviting them to say "yeah, actually." That is fine. If they say yes, ask when to call back. Most say "it's fine, what's up," and now they are participating instead of defending.

What bad sounds like: "Hi, how are you doing today?!" in an upbeat voice. Every telemarketer opens that way. Instant pattern match, instant hangup.

## 2. The reason for the call

**Job:** answer "why me, why now" in one or two sentences. Specific beats general every time.

What good sounds like:

> "The reason I'm calling: I was looking at roofing companies in Fort Worth, and I pulled up your Google listing next to a couple of your competitors. You've got 6 reviews. Two companies near you have over 80. That usually means they're getting the calls you're not."

That works because it proves you did homework on their specific business. You are not calling "businesses." You are calling Mike, about Mike's problem.

What bad sounds like: "I'm reaching out to local businesses in your area to see if you'd be interested in marketing services." No reason for the call at all. That is a reason for a hangup.

Before every call, have one specific observation ready: their review count, an outdated website, no website, a competitor outranking them, a broken contact form. Thirty seconds of research per prospect is enough.

## 3. The value statement

**Job:** connect their situation to a result they care about, in one sentence. Not your features. Their outcome.

What good sounds like:

> "What we do is get home-service companies to the top of Google in their city, so when someone searches 'roofer near me,' they call you instead of the guy with 80 reviews."

Notice what is missing: no "SEO," no "digital marketing solutions," no "full-service agency." A roofer does not want SEO. He wants his phone to ring with jobs.

Rule of thumb: describe the result the owner would brag about to a friend. "My phone rings more" is a result. "I have optimized meta tags" is not.

What bad sounds like: a 45-second monologue listing services. The moment you list more than one thing, you sound like a brochure and they stop listening.

## 4. The discovery question

**Job:** get them talking. A prospect who is talking is a prospect who has not hung up, and their answer tells you which pain to focus on.

What good sounds like:

> "Can I ask, where do most of your jobs come from right now? Mostly referrals and word of mouth?"

Or:

> "When someone in your city searches for a roofer, do you know where you show up? Have you ever looked?"

These questions are easy to answer, non-threatening, and almost always surface a gap. Most home-service businesses run on referrals and know it is fragile. When they say "mostly word of mouth," you can say:

> "That's what I hear from most guys doing good work. The problem is referrals don't scale, and slow months hit hard. That's exactly the gap we fill."

What bad sounds like: interrogating them with five rapid-fire questions, or asking something huge like "what are your business goals for the next five years?" One question. Let them answer fully. Listen for the pain and repeat their words back.

## 5. The ask

**Job:** convert the conversation into a scheduled next step. Small, specific, low pressure.

What good sounds like:

> "Here's what I'd suggest. Give me 15 minutes later this week. I'll pull up your listing and your top two competitors on a screen share and show you exactly what they're doing that you're not. If it's useful, great, and if not, you lose 15 minutes. Does Thursday morning or afternoon work better?"

Why it works:

- **Small ask.** 15 minutes, not an hour.
- **Concrete deliverable.** They walk away with something (the competitor breakdown) even if they never buy.
- **Easy exit named for them.** "If not, you lose 15 minutes" lowers the pressure.
- **A choice between two times,** not "would you ever maybe want to...". Open-ended asks get "send me some info," which is usually a polite no.

What bad sounds like: "So, would you be interested in learning more about our services?" This invites a yes/no, and the default no wins. Never end a good conversation without proposing a specific day and time.

After they agree: confirm their email, tell them you are sending a calendar invite, and send it while you are still on the phone if you can. A meeting that is not on a calendar has a poor chance of happening.

## The whole thing, assembled

> "Hey, is this Mike? Mike, this is Jack. I'll be honest, this is a cold call. Want to give me 30 seconds, then decide if it's worth more? ... The reason I'm calling: I pulled up your Google listing next to two other roofers in Fort Worth. You've got 6 reviews, they've got over 80, and that usually means they're getting calls you're not. What we do is get roofers to the top of Google in their city so those calls come to you. Can I ask, where do most of your jobs come from right now? ... Yeah, that's what most guys tell me, and referrals are great until you hit a slow month. Here's what I'd suggest: 15 minutes Thursday, I'll screen-share your listing next to your competitors and show you exactly what the gap is. If it's not useful, you're out 15 minutes. Morning or afternoon?"

That is the skeleton. Your words will drift from any script within two weeks of real calling, and that is fine. The five stages, in order, each doing its one job, is the part you keep.
